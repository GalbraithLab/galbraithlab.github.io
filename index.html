Quantitative cell biology · OHSU

Mapping the molecular landscape

We build new ways to see individual molecules inside living cells, and use them to discover how molecular organization drives what cells do. One example is the cytoplasmic flows that deliver proteins to the front of a migrating cell.

New from the lab

Nature Communications · 2026

Compartmentalized cytoplasmic tradewinds direct soluble proteins

Galbraith CG, English BP, Boehm U, Galbraith JA

Jim and Cathy, co-corresponding authors

Jim and Cathy discovered that actin treadmilling drives compartmentalized cytoplasmic flows, or “tradewinds,” that carry soluble proteins to the leading edge of migrating cells. This is a previously unknown principle of how cells organize their interior.

Featured in a Current Biology Dispatch by R. Dyche Mullins: “Cytoplasmic rivers carry molecular cargo.”

Nature Methods · 2026

A series of spontaneously blinking dyes for super-resolution microscopy

Holland KL, Plutkis SE, English BP, … Galbraith CG, Lavis LD

To see individual proteins inside living cells, you need to control when fluorescent molecules turn on and off. These spontaneously blinking dyes, made with the Lavis Lab at HHMI Janelia, do that without harsh chemicals or damaging light.

Research

What we study

Cytoplasmic transport

How cytoplasmic “tradewinds” deliver soluble proteins to where, and when, a migrating cell needs them.

Directed cell migration

How the molecular organization of adhesions and the actin cytoskeleton turns matrix probing into directed movement.

Fluorescent probes

Spontaneously blinking dyes that let us see individual proteins in living cells, without harsh chemicals or damaging light.

Custom microscopes

Microscopes we design and build ourselves, to make measurements that were previously impossible.
